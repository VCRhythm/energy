Cellular Automata

Life & computation from a grid

A cellular automaton is a grid of cells, each switching on or off based only on its neighbors. There is no central controller — yet these rules produce gliders, oscillators, self-replication, and even universal computation. Two classics below.

Conway's Game of Life

Four rules decide each cell's fate: a live cell with 2–3 live neighbors survives, a dead cell with exactly 3 neighbors is born, everything else dies. Draw on the grid, drop in a pattern, and watch the population breathe.

Elementary (Wolfram) Automata

One dimension, two states, and a rule that maps each cell's left–center–right neighborhood to its next value. There are exactly 256 such rules. Each new generation is drawn as a row below the last, building a space-time diagram.

Currently showing Rule 30. Rule 30 is chaotic (used as a randomness source), Rule 90 draws the Sierpiński triangle, and Rule 110 is proven Turing-complete — capable, in principle, of any computation. All from a 1-D row of bits.
