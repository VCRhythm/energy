Emergence & Agents

Order without a designer

In agent-based models, every individual follows its own small set of local rules. Nobody is in charge — but global structure emerges anyway: flocks, segregated neighborhoods, and avalanches poised at criticality.

Boids — Flocking

Craig Reynolds showed in 1986 that lifelike flocking needs just three steering rules: cohesion (move toward neighbors), alignment (match their heading), and separation (don't crowd). Tune the balance and watch the flock's character change.

Try this: crank separation to 0 and the boids clump into a single dot; zero out cohesion and alignment and they scatter like a gas. The flock lives in the balance between.

Schelling's Segregation Model

Each agent is content as long as at least a fraction of its neighbors share its color; unhappy agents relocate to a random empty cell. The shock: even a mild preference (wanting just 30% similar neighbors) drives near-total segregation. Individual tolerance ≠ collective outcome.

Forest Fire — Self-Organized Criticality

Trees grow at random; rare lightning ignites a tree and burns its whole connected stand. The forest tunes itself to a critical density where fires of every size occur — and their sizes follow a power law: countless tiny fires, a few catastrophic ones. The same statistics describe earthquakes and avalanches.
